## Interaction and accessibility review

The full first run recorded all seven keyboard keys, semantic section labels, skip link, focus outline, minimum button targets, audio toggle/persistence, replay, fallback selection, orientation changes, memory loops, and adaptation as passing their automated checks. Console warnings/errors, page errors, and external-origin request arrays were empty.

Two first-iteration results needed interpretation and fixes:

1. The pointer-parallax assertion failed. Code review found that camera rotations were applied before a final look-at, which overwrote them. Rotation was moved after the final look-at; the completed final pointer/cursor assertion now passes.
2. The touch shockwave check recorded a count change from 0 to 2 even though its loose assertion passed. Pointerdown plus pointerup caused duplicate activation. The handler now fires once on pointerup after a short-movement tap and excludes buttons/links. The final count increases from 0 to 1, confirming the duplicate trigger is removed.

A context-loss overlay class mismatch was corrected. Reduced-motion camera parallax is disabled independently of pointer tracking, preserving Core proximity input. The fallback replay now traverses the page over 40 seconds, with chapter steps for reduced motion; fallback keyboard controls and return-to-start were exercised separately.

The final desktop M9 image and both final contact sheets have materially better focal separation and readable CTA composition. The hard scrim rectangles seen in the baseline are absent in the final panels. This visual improvement does not establish quantitative accessibility contrast: the separate conservative measurement fails, as recorded below.

## Performance evidence and verification limits

First-iteration renderer calls stayed below 120 in the 22 captured low-tier samples; the largest recorded triangle count was approximately 1.20 million. The report identifies ANGLE SwiftShader, with software sample FPS approximately 7–33 and an average around 17.3. These are not physical GPU results.

The first-run geometry/texture counts stabilized through five full scroll loops. Desktop CLS was approximately 0.0026 and mobile CLS stayed near zero. Mobile software rendering recorded long tasks after loading; the strict no-long-task target was not established. Five scroll loops are also not the same as a five-minute physical-device memory soak.

## Final rerun outcome

The completed [final report](report.json) contains **42 passing assertions, zero failed assertions, 22 samples, and two performance observations**. Console warnings/errors, page errors, and external-origin requests are all zero. Actual rendering used **Intel UHD Graphics 730 through ANGLE Direct3D11**; it was not the earlier SwiftShader path.

All 22 final contact-sheet panels were reviewed. Arrival/Atmosphere retain the cloud and surface detail, with complete hero silhouette at p0. The ring plane and gate field remain readable spatially; the p0.52 transition is still comparatively sparse. Nebula now has dark cavities and warm internal density instead of uniform pale cyan. The p0.90 approach is still brighter than the closing pose but no longer nearly washed out. At p1, the energy heart is distinctly visible inside dark liquid glass on desktop and mobile. Mobile headings, body copy, and CTA remain inside the viewport. Neither final sheet shows the earlier hard rectangle edges around the scrims.

## Contrast remains a failure

[contrast-report.json](contrast-report.json) hides foreground text while retaining its scrim, then takes the minimum WCAG contrast ratio across each entire DOM text rectangle, including spaces. It is deliberately conservative and measures one animated frame per sample; it is not a glyph-by-glyph contrast mask.

**27 of 42 rectangles fall below 4.5:1. AC-051 is FAIL.** Examples include the desktop hero subtitle (1.18:1 conservative bound), Atmosphere body (1.70:1), and Rings body (2.20:1). Some mobile body/headline rectangles pass, but cyan labels and bright particles still lower the conservative bounds. The scrims retain the specification's maximum 0.55 opacity. No claim is made that every affected glyph is unreadable, and no claim is made that the required worst-case contrast guarantee is satisfied.

Build/tooling checks and scripted browser checks are complete; remaining material differences and performance limits are listed in [DEVIATIONS.md](../DEVIATIONS.md). All-AC success is not claimed. This documentation review launched no competing browser during the final suite.
